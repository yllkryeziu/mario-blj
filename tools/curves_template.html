Shaping the Backwards Long Jump

The staircase loops Mario back down unless he crosses a trigger zone inside a single frame, which needs a speed no ordinary move produces. So reaching the top certifies the exploit. The question was how much reward shaping an agent needs to get there. The answer turns out to be none, and what shaping buys is speed and reliability rather than possibility.

Mean episode return

The ordinary reward curve, one panel per rung. Each panel has its own y axis because each rung can pay a different maximum, though they now share a unit: reaching the top landing pays 1.0 in every rung and each shaping term adds at most 0.25 on top, so terminal caps at 1.00, speed and height at 1.25, and the two together at 1.50. Thin lines are seeds, the heavy line is the median.

The same curves, normalized

Each rung's median return divided by the best mean return that rung ever reached, which puts the four shapes on one axis regardless of their ceilings. Height shaping is the one to look at: it climbs fast, saturates near its own ceiling, and stays there. Its reward goes flat exactly where ordinary movement stops working, so there is no gradient across the discontinuity the exploit lives on.

Steps to the first success

One marker per seed. A hollow marker sits at the end of that run's budget and means the exploit was never found. Shaping moves the whole row left.

Success rate over training

Fraction of episodes in each 250k step bin that reached the top landing. Thin lines are individual seeds, the heavy line is the median across them. Runs end where their budget ended.

The numbers as a table
These are the normalized reward runs. An earlier version paid 0.01 per unit of record backwards speed without a ceiling, which at a peak of -6000 paid about 59 against a goal worth 1.0, so the agent was paid sixty times more for going fast than for finishing. Every shaping term is now a bounded fraction of the goal, and the speed term saturates at the speed that actually defeats the loop rather than rewarding speed for its own sake. That one change took speed shaping from 2 of 3 seeds to 6 of 6, and moved its fastest discovery from 3.7M steps to 0.58M. All twenty four runs completed at 20M steps per seed, so the comparison is even. The curriculum rung is excluded: it pays for each stage of a hand written recipe, which names the method rather than the goal, so succeeding at it says only that the answer was supplied. A fifth rung swept action repeat and is excluded, because it was measuring the wrong thing. Re-launching the chain needs INPUT_A_PRESSED, which never re-latches on a held button, so holding one action for k frames forces a minimum A press period of 2k. Measured with this project's scripted expert: period 2 reaches -715, period 4 reaches -31.6, period 6 reaches -20.8. Above a repeat of 1 the exploit is not harder to learn, it cannot be expressed, and all nine runs confirmed it with zero successes. Success rate is measured per bin, so a rate of 1.0 means every episode in that 250k step window reached the top. Seeds that never solve contribute a flat zero, which is why a median can sit at zero while one seed is at one.
